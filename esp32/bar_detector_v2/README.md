# bar_detector_v2: which bar rang, 38 kHz or 47 kHz? (prototype 2, PDM MEMS mic)

Prototype 2 of the bar detector. It runs on its own LilyGO T-Display: MAC `30:c6:f7:25:ad:f0` since 2026-10-23. That board replaced
`a0:dd:6c:71:14:04`, whose screen was faulty.
- **Sensor:** a digital MEMS microphone (Elecrow module, **Syntiant/Knowles SPH0641LU4H-1**), instead of
  prototype 1's analog receiver on GPIO32.
- **How it works:** the firmware reads the mic's raw 1-bit PDM stream, turns it into 192 kS/s PCM, and
  runs prototype 1's detector (`lib/tone_detector`, unchanged) on it.
- **Prototype 1** stays in [`../bar_detector/`](../bar_detector) and is not modified.

| Strike | Screen |
|---|---|
| ~38 kHz bar | **blue** tile, `38 kHz`, measured frequency |
| ~47 kHz bar (detected by its 42.78 kHz line) | **orange** tile, `47 kHz`, measured frequency |
| both bands within 10 dB | **grey** tile, `38+47?` (ambiguous; never forced) |
| nothing | `LISTENING`, with one live SNR meter per band |

## Status (2026-10-23)

- **Board:** flashed on the new board. The mic answers: `ones=0.500` and `rate_meas=192003`.
- **CPU load:** `load_pct` ~64 (the decimator takes ~31 of it), and `load_max_pct` is 68.
- **Word order:** the DMA delivers the PDM words in time order, so `swap=0` is right. With `swap=1`, the
  broadband RMS rises from -55 to -26 dBFS.
- **Band centres:** re-measured with this mic using `spec on` (see `lib/device_config/DeviceConfig.h`),
  with tolerance ±300 Hz:
  - **A, 38 618 Hz:** the ~38 kHz bar rang at 38 595.5 Hz when struck. Its sympathetic rings, while
    the other bar was struck, were at 38 606-38 650 Hz. Second line ~33.94 kHz. τ ≈ 39 ms, Q ≈ 4 800.
  - **B, 47 088 Hz:** the other bar, median of 11 strikes (47 081-47 114 Hz). Second line ~45.37 kHz.
    τ ≈ 31 ms, Q ≈ 4 600. Prototype 1's 42.78 kHz line (its old band B) was not present.
- **Background:** a 38.00 kHz source (FSK-remote-like, with a faint 42.7-43.2 kHz line) sounds without
  any impact click. It fades in the room's reverberation, which the detector took for a ringdown while
  band A sat at 38.0 kHz ±500 Hz. A is now ~600 Hz above it, with a ±300 Hz band.
- **Verified on the board** (same day, background source still on):
  - **The 47 bar:** 15 of 15 strikes gave B, at 64-90 dB with a 14-69 dB margin.
  - **The 38 bar:** 5 of 5 strikes gave A, at 66-76 dB with a 14-60 dB margin, measured at
    38 596-38 611 Hz.
  - **Background:** no false detections over 3 min. A few weak onsets (20-24 dB) did not ring down and
    were rejected (`confirmed=0`, serial only).
- **Sympathetic ringing:** each bar makes the other ring. Twice the other band reached within
  14-17 dB of the struck one. A strike where it gets within 10 dB would show the grey `38+47?` tile.
- **One-off processing cost:** the two FFTs of a `spec` capture take ~23 ms in one buffer
  (`load_max_pct` 290). The 64 ms DMA queue absorbs this, and `load_pct` stays at 63-65.

## Wiring

| Mic module | T-Display |
|---|---|
| VDD | 3V3 (the mic takes 1.62-3.6 V) |
| GND | GND |
| CLK | **GPIO22** (the clock is generated by the ESP32) |
| DATA | **GPIO21** |
| SEL (L/R) | GND |

With SEL at GND, the mic drives DATA after the falling clock edge and the host latches it on the rising
edge. That is the edge the ESP32's I2S receiver samples on, so nothing needs inverting.

GPIO0 (BOOT button) must stay free. The code sets `mck_io_num = I2S_PIN_NO_CHANGE`; left at 0, GPIO0
would output MCLK.

## How the PDM stream becomes PCM

The SPH0641 has four modes, chosen by its clock:

| Clock | Mode |
|---|---|
| < 250 kHz | sleep |
| 351-815 kHz | low power |
| 1.024-2.475 MHz | standard |
| **3.072-4.8 MHz** | **ultrasonic** (response to 80 kHz, specified at 3.072 MHz) |

Each clock cycle carries one bit, and the density of ones follows the sound pressure. Sensitivity is
-26 dBFS at 94 dB SPL, so full scale is ~120 dB SPL (also its overload point).

- **Why not the ESP32's own PDM decoder:** it decimates by 64 or 128 only. At 3.072 MHz that gives
  48 kS/s, whose Nyquist frequency is below both bars.
- **What the firmware does instead:**
  - **Raw capture:** I2S0 runs as a plain 32-bit stereo master receiver. Its BCK is the mic clock,
    3.072 MHz (frame rate 48 000 × 64 BCK, APLL). Every clock cycle lands in a slot, so the DMA
    delivers the raw bitstream, first bit at bit 31.
  - **Decimation by 16 to 192 kS/s** (Nyquist 96 kHz), in [`lib/pdm_decimator`](lib/pdm_decimator):
    - a 256-tap linear-phase FIR (Kaiser-windowed sinc, β = 9, cutoff 96 kHz), evaluated once every
      16 bits with one 256-entry lookup table per byte of the filter (32 lookups per output);
    - the response is flat within 0.01 dB to 60 kHz, -0.7 dB at 80 kHz, and ≤ -90 dB from 132 kHz
      up. 132 kHz is the lowest frequency that would fold back into 0-60 kHz;
    - the output is in full-scale units (±1 = 0 dBFS).
- **Wake-up order:** the datasheet forbids waking from sleep straight into ultrasonic mode, and the mic
  sleeps whenever the ESP32 resets (no clock). `startMic()` therefore clocks it at **2.4 MHz**
  (standard mode) for 100 ms, then switches to **3.072 MHz**.
- **Detector:** [`lib/device_config`](lib/device_config) holds the settings that the firmware and the
  tests share. The detector runs on 1536-sample blocks: 8.0 ms and 125 Hz bins, against prototype 1's
  8.2 ms and 122 Hz. Its thresholds therefore keep their meaning. How it decides is described in
  [`../bar_detector/README.md`](../bar_detector/README.md#how-it-detects).

## Screens

As in prototype 1, with two differences:
- **Bottom line:** `rms .. pk .. dBFS 192k` replaces DC/Vpp. It shows the RMS of the last 8 ms and the
  peak over the last 100 ms, both in dBFS.
- **Status:**
  - `NO MIC` (red) means the data line is stuck: no supply, no clock, or DATA not connected;
  - `CLIP` means a sample reached -1 dBFS.

**Right button (GPIO35):** reset the baselines and counters, then learn for 1 s.

## Flashing and serial

```bash
cd ~/signal_analysis/esp32/bar_detector_v2
pio run -t upload        # board on /dev/ttyACM0 (usbipd BUSID 3-2 on the current port)
```

The serial link runs at 921600 baud. Opening the port resets the board, so wait for `READY`.

| Command | Reply |
|---|---|
| `info` | `INFO rate=.. rate_meas=.. pdm_clk=.. decim=.. swap=.. block=.. bin_hz=.. band_a=.. band_b=.. tol_hz=.. thr_db=.. hits_a=.. hits_b=.. hits_both=.. ones=.. rms_dbfs=.. peak_dbfs=.. load_pct=.. load_max_pct=.. dec_pct=..` |
| `lvl <on\|off>` | `LVL on=..`, then ~20 lines/s: `LVL snr_a=.. snr_b=.. guard_a=.. guard_b=.. level_a=.. level_b=.. rms_dbfs=.. learning=.. active=.. clip=..` |
| `thr <dB>` | `THR db=..` (6-40 dB; relearns) |
| `band <a\|b> <Hz>` | `BAND a=.. b=..` (relearns) |
| `pdm` | `PDM ones=.. rms_dbfs=.. peak_dbfs=.. swap=..` (`ones` ~0.5 when the mic talks) |
| `pdm swap <0\|1>` | flips the DMA word order (bring-up only; `swap=0` is right on this board) |
| `spec <on\|off>` | `SPEC on=..`; while on, after each strike: `SPEC t=.. trig_db=.. floor_dbfs=.. peaks=..`, then one `SPEC_PEAK f=.. early_dbfs=.. late_dbfs=.. drop_db=..` per line, strongest first |
| `reset` | `RESET ok=1` (same as the right button) |

Unsolicited lines are `READY`, then `EVT phase=detect|end ...`, exactly as in prototype 1.

`level_a` / `level_b` are in dBFS. Add ~120 for a rough dB SPL; the mic is calibrated at 1 kHz, not
in the ultrasonic range.

### `spec`: where does a bar actually ring?

The detector only watches two 1 kHz bands. `spec on` ([`lib/strike_spectrum`](lib/strike_spectrum))
finds the lines of an unknown or changed bar anywhere in 20-90 kHz.
- **Trigger:** each 8 ms buffer's ultrasonic energy is compared with its own running baseline. The
  energy is the mean square of the second difference, which suppresses audio by 60 dB at 1 kHz. A rise
  of 10 dB or more (`trig_db`), the impact click, starts a capture.
- **Two FFTs:** 4096 points each, Hann window, 46.9 Hz bins:
  - **early:** 10 ms after the start of the triggering buffer, that is 2-10 ms after the click;
  - **late:** 40 ms later.
- **Lines reported:** up to 6 local maxima of the early spectrum, at least 15 dB above its median
  (`floor_dbfs`). Frequencies are interpolated to a few Hz, and levels are given as sine amplitudes in
  dBFS.
- **`drop_db` = early - late:**
  - a ringing mode decays: τ ≈ 0.347 / `drop_db` s (amplitude time constant), so Q = π f τ;
  - a steady line (environment, remote) shows `drop_db` ≈ 0.
- **Re-arm:** 300 ms after a capture.
- **Cost:** about 90 KB of RAM, allocated in `setup()` (`heap_free` ~157 KB left). The CPU cost is
  negligible except for the two FFTs once per strike.

## Tests (on the PC, no board)

```bash
pio test -e native
```

- **`test/test_pdm`**: test signals go through a 2nd-order sigma-delta modulator at 3.072 MHz, then the
  decimator, and, for the chain tests, the detector with the firmware's settings. It checks:
  - the filter response;
  - that both DMA word orders give the same PCM;
  - that chunked and one-shot decimation match;
  - silence;
  - the level and frequency of a 40 kHz tone;
  - the rejection of a 150 kHz alias;
  - full-chain strikes: A, B, a weak strike, A then B;
  - that clicks, the FSK remote, and a strike after the remote are handled.
- **`test/test_spectrum`**: the strike spectrum (`spec`):
  - the FFT against a direct DFT;
  - two decaying modes plus a steady line: frequencies within 5 Hz, `drop_db` matching τ within 1 dB,
    the steady line's drop ≈ 0, and no false peaks;
  - noise alone does not trigger;
  - two strikes give two captures;
  - a weak strike is found through its click.
- **`test/test_detector`**: prototype 1's detector tests, unchanged, including `rec4.wav` / `rec5.wav`.
  `lib/tone_detector` must stay identical to prototype 1's: `diff -r ../bar_detector/lib/tone_detector
  lib/tone_detector`.

Every change to `lib/` needs these tests to pass, plus a new test for the behaviour it changes.
